The problem: shared money, unclear control

A birthday billboard is a community effort. When its budget sits in an organizer’s personal wallet, supporters depend on updates to understand where it can go. FanPot makes the recipient, spending limit and approval process inspectable before anyone contributes.

Three rules supporters can check

  • Commit first. The goal, deadline, recipient addresses and payout caps are fixed before funding. An existing campaign cannot be reconfigured.
  • Review before payment. Once funding succeeds, the organizer requests a payout; a separate reviewer wallet approves the exact amount and evidence hash. The contract pays the committed recipient directly.
  • Return eligible balances. Supporters can claim refunds if funding fails, or their share of unused funds after settlement. Claims require a transaction; gas is not refunded.

A global audience. A focused starting point.

225 million reported Hallyu fans in 2023 [1]. About $120 million in Korean CD exports across 131 destinations in Q1 2026 [2]. These show broad cultural reach and cross-border purchasing; they do not measure FanPot’s addressable market or fan-ad spending.

First users: birthday-ad organizers sharing campaign links with existing international fan communities. FanPlus already runs recurring birthday and anniversary votes with advertising rewards [3]. FanPot’s opportunity is to make the pooled budget itself verifiable.

What makes FanPot different?

  • FanPlus: voting that earns advertising rewards [3].
  • MAKESTAR: reward crowdfunding with platform-managed funding and refund policies [4].
  • FanPot: fan-organized USDC funding with immutable recipient caps, a reviewer approval gate, and publicly inspectable receipts.

The distinction is how spending authority is enforced. Existing services have their own safeguards. FanPot puts campaign constraints in a non-upgradeable contract with no organizer withdrawal override.

Why Arc + USDC belong in the product

  • One asset to prepare. Arc uses USDC for gas, so a supporter can fund the campaign and pay network fees with USDC [5].
  • One accounting unit. Goals, contributions, capped payouts and refunds share the same dollar-denominated asset across participating wallets.
  • Enforceable rules, inspectable state. Arc’s EVM execution runs the escrow; the live page reads campaign totals from the chain. Public receipts let supporters verify money movement.

Executed end to end on Arc Mainnet

Funding → organizer request → reviewer-approved payment → settlement → supporter refund. Every step has a confirmed Mainnet transaction. The campaign is now closed, with all contributed USDC accounted for.

Verify the full transaction trail · Reviewer-approved payment · Completed refund. Run pnpm verify:proof to reproduce the checks.

Current scope: early proof capped at 100 USDC per campaign. Artists are fictional, imagery is AI-generated, and all demonstration wallets are builder-controlled; no independent fan traction or fulfilled ad booking is claimed. The contracts are not externally audited. Evidence hashes record commitments, not proof of delivery.

Next: validate one campaign with a real organizer, independent reviewer and recipient; conduct a security review before raising limits. Measure contribution completion and reconciliation time. Fiat onboarding and vendor acceptance remain work to do.
